# Grammar terms

How Ibn Ezra's grammar terms are translated: the rules, then the glossary itself (Hebrew → the English used in the book), taken from your "Ibn Ezra Grammar Organizer" sheet. The numbers ("Ruling 22") are entries in **03 Rulings**. The last part, "For Claude", says how it is checked.

## The rules

### 1. One English for each term, from your grammar sheet
**Rule.** Every grammar term is translated by the glossary below, the same way every time. Where Ibn Ezra uses a term in his own medieval sense, his sense wins, and the key to terms marks it with ◆.

**Example.** קָמָץ קָטָן is "small kamatz" — Ibn Ezra's name for the tzere — marked ◆ in the key, because in modern grammar the name means something else.

**Why.** A reader who learns a term on one page meets the same English on every other page.

### 2. The key to terms and the foot strip match the translation
**Rule.** Every term a reader may not know goes into the key to terms (front matter) and the definitions at the foot of the page. The key's English is the same as the translation's.

**Why.** The reader looks a word up and finds the very word on the page.

### 3. When unsure, ask in the review
**Rule.** If Claude is not sure how a term or a grammar point should be translated — the sheet and Ibn Ezra seem to disagree, a term is not on the sheet, or a passage can be read two ways — it is never settled by guessing. The best reading goes into the note, stated plainly (no "Uncertain" label: **04 Translation**, rule 4), and a row goes into the review list's grammar Part: where, the Hebrew line, the rendering now, the alternative, and in plain words why Claude is unsure. Your answer is then added to the glossary.

**Why.** Grammar terms are translation choices, and translation choices are yours.

### 4. Places you have already decided stay as you decided
**Rule.** Where a term at one place was put to you and you chose to keep the wording there, it stays, even where the glossary would say otherwise.

**Example.** Bereshit 1:1 "the discerning will understand" and 1:14 "superfluous" stayed as they were.

**Why.** Your answer at that place is the ruling for that place. *Ruling 28.*

## The glossary (Hebrew → English used in the book)

- Parts of speech:
  - שֵם noun, name (also הַשֵּם = Hashem, see **04 Translation**, rule 9).
  - פּוֹעַל verb (also the active participle).
  - מִלָּה word (key to terms: just "word"; Ibn Ezra's מִלַּת X means "the word X"; Ruling 23).
  - מִלַּת עִנְיָן word of relation (a small word that links or relates rather than names a thing, as הֵן, עִם; Ruling 24).
  - כִּנּוּי pronoun, pronominal suffix; also a byname or euphemism.
  - תּוֹאַר / שֵם הַתּוֹאַר adjective — always simply "adjective", also at 1:1 אֱלֹהִים ("an adjective, not a proper noun") and at 1:26 נַעֲשֶׂה (Ruling 22).
  - שֹׁרֶשׁ root (three-letter base).
- Tenses and forms: עָבָר past · עָתִיד future · צִוּוּי imperative (command) · מָקוֹר and Ibn Ezra's שֵם הַפּוֹעַל infinitive · פּוֹעֵל active participle · פָּעוּל passive participle · פּוֹעַל יוֹצֵא transitive verb · פּוֹעַל עוֹמֵד intransitive verb.
- Conjugations (בִּנְיָנִים):
  - קַל / הַבִּנְיָן הַקַּל = the light conjugation (kal, simple).
  - נִפְעַל = nif'al (passive/reflexive).
  - פִּעֵל and Ibn Ezra's הַבִּנְיָן הַכָּבֵד = the heavy conjugation, pi'el (intensive) ◆.
  - הִפְעִיל and Ibn Ezra's הַכָּבֵד הַנּוֹסָף = the heavy conjugation with an added letter, hif'il (causative) ◆.
  - הִתְפַּעֵל = hitpa'el (reflexive).
  - בִּנְיָן שֶׁלֹּא נִקְרָא שֵׁם פּוֹעֲלוֹ = "(the conjugation) whose agent is not named", i.e. the passive.
- Root letters: פּ"א / ע"י / ל"מ הַפּוֹעַל first / middle / last root letter · weak roots פּ"נ, פּ"י, פּ"א, ע"ו, ע"י, ל"ה, ל"א = "roots whose first/middle/last letter is X" · כְּפוּלִים doubled roots · מִגִּזְרַת from the root of · מִשְׁקָל pattern · Ibn Ezra's הַפְּעָלִים הַשְּׁנִיִּים הַנִּרְאִים = "the verbs whose middle root letter is ו" (Lech Lecha 17:10; Ruling 25).
- Weak and silent letters: גְּרוּנִיּוֹת guttural letters (א ה ח ע) · אוֹתִיּוֹת אֶהוָ"י weak letters (א ה ו י) · נָח / נָחִים quiescent (silent) letter(s) · נָח נֶעְלָם hidden quiescent letter · נֶעְלָמִים dropped (unpronounced) letters.
- Letter changes: הַבְלָעָה absorption · חִלּוּף / מִתְחַלְּפִים substitution, interchange (letters that take each other's place) · תַּחַת in place of · הִתְמַזְּגוּת merging · הִדּוּמָה assimilation · נָפַל dropped · נוֹסָף added (not of the root; not "superfluous") · חָסֵר missing · יָתֵר extra · דָּגֵשׁ dagesh · רָפֶה without dagesh (soft) · שְׁוָא נָח silent sheva · שְׁוָא נָע mobile sheva · סִימַן הַמְדַבֵּר the sign of the speaker (the prefix for "I").
- Vowels: פַּתָּח patach · קָמָץ kamatz · צֵירֵי tzere · סֶגּוֹל segol · חִירִיק hirik · חוֹלָם holam · שׁוּרוּק shuruk · קָבּוּץ kubutz · שְׁוָא sheva (written in the English with its sign on an alef: **04 Translation**, rule 14).
  - Ibn Ezra's own names: קָמָץ גָּדוֹל "large kamatz" (= kamatz) · קָמָץ קָטָן "small kamatz" (= tzere) ◆ · פַּתָּח גָּדוֹל "large patach" (= patach) · פַּתָּח קָטָן "small patach" (= segol) ◆.
  - Translate his name literally, and give the modern name the first time it appears on a page or in the note.
- Accents: where Ibn Ezra names an accent whose sense is unclear, give the name in Hebrew and the literal English together: עִנְיַן מוּכְרָת ("a matter cut off") (Lech Lecha 12:19; Ruling 26).
- Syntax: סָמוּךְ construct (state), bound to the next word · נִפְרָד absolute (independent) form · טַעַם sense, meaning; also reason, and an accent (◆, e.g. בַּעַל הַטְּעָמִים "the master of the accents") · חַסְר מִלָּה an implied (understood) word · מוֹשֵׁךְ / מְשָׁרֵת עַצְמוֹ וְאַחֵר עִמּוֹ draws / serves itself and another with it (one word applies twice) · דֶּרֶךְ קְצָרָה a short way of speaking · לְשׁוֹן רַבִּים / יָחִיד plural / singular · זָכָר / נְקֵבָה masculine / feminine (in grammar).
- Reading and interpretation: דֶּרֶךְ הַפְּשָׁט the plain (literal) sense · **דְּרָשׁ homiletical interpretation** (not "homily"; Ruling 27; in the key to terms and the foot strip: "an exposition of a Torah passage that draws out a deeper, often moral, theological, or conceptual meaning beyond its plain sense"; the strip label is "homiletical interpretation (derash)") · דֶּרֶךְ הַדִּקְדּוּק the way of grammar · כְּמִשְׁפָּט according to (the) rule · אֵין לוֹ אָח it has no other like it (a unique form in Scripture) · הֶקְשֵׁר context · סוֹד secret.
- Signature phrases (always the same English): יֵשׁ אוֹמְרִים / י"א some say · וַאֲחֵרִים אָמְרוּ and others said · וְהַמַּשְׂכִּיל יָבִין and the wise will understand · כְּמִשְׁפָּט according to rule · פֵּירוּשׁ / פי' its explanation is · כְּמַשְׁמָעָם as they sound (literally) · וְהַנָּכוֹן בְּעֵינַי what is correct in my eyes · וְהַיָּשָׁר בְּעֵינַי what is straight in my eyes · קַדְמוֹנֵינוּ ז"ל our early sages, of blessed memory · הַשֵּׁם הַנִּכְבָּד the Honored Name · דֶּרֶךְ קְצָרָה a short way of speaking. His formulas לֹא יִתָּכֵן and וְהָעֵד שֶׁאָמַר take the English that reads best in each sentence (Ruling 106).

## For Claude: how it is done and checked

- The grammar sheet is kept in the project as ref/grammar_sheet.png; if it is missing, ask David for it. Check every term against it before translating, and again before each draft.
- Glossary entry 1 and rule 2: the foot-of-page definitions are in data/foot_terms.json of each parasha (page.md, rule 9); the key to terms is in bereshit/data/front.json.
- **Before each draft** run `python3 scripts/terms_audit.py <model.json>` in the parasha folder (scripts/check_book.py runs it too; Bereshit's model is data/model_final5.json, the others' data/model.json). For every term it finds in the Hebrew, it checks that the English of the same verse uses the house rendering, and lists the places that don't and the terms rendered more than one way. Read each one. A real inconsistency becomes a review-list row, not a silent change, because it is a translation change. The places of rule 4 are expected hits.
- Rule 3: the grammar Part was Part G in the Bereshit list and Part C in Noach's and Lech Lecha's; if a list has none, create one. Each row gives where, the Hebrew line, Now, Proposal and why Claude is unsure.
