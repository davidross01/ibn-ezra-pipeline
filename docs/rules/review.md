# House rules — Review process

Part of commentary-house-rules (the core is the skill itself). Moved here word for word on 27 Oct 2026; change a rule here and in the core line that points to it.

## Review process
- **When David asks to add or change something, never build a new draft. Add it to the review as a row, even if he does not say "review".** (David's rule, Oct 2026.) This covers a new rule, a mistake he found, a wording change, an extra note, or a layout change. The row follows the usual layout: where, the Hebrew line, Now, Proposal, Decision chip, Your note, and an Explanations-tab entry with links. Say in one line which row it is. If a change affects the whole book, one row covers it and lists the places; it may go into whichever review list is open (the Bereshit and Noach place names went into Noach Review List 2, Part C). (Exceptions: when David directly asks to fix the layout or how the PDF looks, or tells you how a sign or bracket should be printed, fix it and send a new draft; when he gives a plain instruction such as "remove these from the notes" or "add this as well" while asking for a draft, do it and say so; when you ask him with checkboxes whether to apply a change now and he says yes, as he did for the Ramban/Strickman removal, the Rashbam lines in Bereshit's final, and the Onkelos lines and ה׳ fix of 27 Oct 2026; and when he says to put a change into the parashot and rebuild them with a review sheet alongside, or to apply and rebuild, as for the grammar notes and the editorial clean-up.)
- **Every review list has an Ideas Part** (David, 30 Oct 2026: "always have them in a section of the review so I can see if I want to keep them"). Every note paragraph that expands one of Ibn Ezra's ideas, his science or a hint (notes.md, "Ibn Ezra's ideas") is put to David as its own row in a Part of its own ("Ibn Ezra's ideas"), with Keep / Approve, even in a new parasha's draft 1: **Since 8 Nov 2026** (David: “On toledot you have made section F all really long, I want them in the same style that you have done in other parashot. And for future parashot I want to show what it would be like without these additions and then an alternative with them”): the draft is built **without** the additions, and each added sentence is its own short row, in the style of the Ideas Audits — **Now** = the note without it (the sentence before and after the place), **Alternative** = the same with the sentence added. The rows follow the fixed order (notes.md): the idea and what a reader would think, Ibn Ezra by Ibn Ezra, Saadia, Rambam; rows that fill the same place are anchored on the sentence after it, so approved rows go in in ID order. Each row names its source, read on Sefaria. Toledot List 1 Part F (F1–F18) and the Ideas Audits are the models; the older one-paragraph rows (Audit 2 Part F) are not.
- **Every parasha's review list has a Place names row** (David, 25 Oct 2026): all place names in English form, with their verses, the transliteration proposed, and Keep / Approve / Change. Lech Lecha Review List 1 Part F is the model. Add it to the list that is built with draft 1 of each new parasha.
- **One row per item, never several in one row** (David, 2 Nov 2026: "you have condensed things into one block, I want it all expanded, D2"). Each misprint, variant, name or change is its own row, with its own Hebrew, English and Decision; a summary row that bundles several (like Chayei Sarah D2, which became G1–G4) is not made.
- **Every parasha's review list has the names of people, one row each, with an alternative spelling** (David, 2 Nov 2026: "for names of people make sure to have a [row] for their name and their alternative, for example Rebekah and Rebecca"). Now = the name as it stands, with where it appears; Alternative = the other spelling (the Hebrew form or the other English one). Lists of names in one verse range (sons of Keturah, sons of Ishmael) may share a row. Names with no second spelling in use are listed in the doc's closing section. Chayei Sarah Review List 1 Part H is the model. A name that also stands in earlier parashot changes there only through its own row. **A name David has already confirmed is not asked again** (David, 5 Nov 2026: “if you have already named them before and i have confirmed it you do not need to ask again about them, just continue in the way we have been naming them”): it keeps the confirmed spelling without a row. The confirmed names, with the row that settled each, are in `docs/rules/confirmed_names.json`; add to it whenever David answers a names row, and only new names get a row. review_audit/make_rows.py stops on a Part H row for a confirmed name.
- **Every Explanation shows the Hebrew and the text both ways** (David's standing preference, enforced 4 Nov 2026 in review_audit/make_rows.py): the Hebrew line (Ibn Ezra's, or the verse when the row gives none), then the sentence as it stands and as it would be if changed, each with English. A row with no ops (made by hand) must give `changed` — the text as it would read — and `changed_he` where Hebrew changes; make_rows prints EXPLANATION WARNINGS otherwise, and they are fixed before the doc is built.
- **The Now box shows the Hebrew it renders** (David, 8 Nov 2026, on Toledot List 1 B10: “in the review, make sure that it shows the Hebrew in the box when it is relevant … that way i can read the hebrew and make the decision on which version”). Wherever Now and Alternative are two renderings of a Hebrew text — a verse translation (Part A) or Ibn Ezra's English (smoothing, grammar terms) — the Now box opens with that Hebrew on its own line, pointed, with no Latin letters in it so the doc sets it right to left: the whole verse, or the sentences of Ibn Ezra's comment that the English excerpt renders (his lemma first, “…” where it is cut). review_audit/make_rows.py does it for every op on `verses.en` or `ec`, taking Ibn Ezra's sentences by their place in the comment; when that is not the right passage, or the comment has no section of its own, the row sets `heb_box` (a marker such as `{hcw:c:v:i-j}`) — Toledot B2 and B4 are the models — and make_rows warns where it found none. Rows that add or change a note (Parts D and F, the Ideas Audits) keep the Hebrew in the Explanations tab, where it already is. Toledot List 1 was updated in place the same day (A1–A2, B1–B11, C1–C2), answers untouched.
- **Discuss rows**: explain the row in plain words (the Hebrew, what each choice means, a real example), then ask the decision with checkboxes in the same turn, and apply the answer with the others (Front Matter C5 and Lech Lecha C2, 25 Oct 2026). Where a Change note can be read two ways, ask with checkboxes before applying (Noach A1, Lech Lecha B6).
- A new draft is built only when David asks for a draft, or after he has answered rows and says to apply them (apply-review-decisions), or for a layout fix he asked for. Do not change the book, the model data or the glossary otherwise.
- Every open question goes to David as a row in the review-list Google Doc (review-list-google-doc skill): ID, where, issue, Now, Alternative, Decision chip, Your note, Explanation.
- **One review document per parasha, archived when finished** (David, row D2, Oct 2026). Start a new, short review doc for each parasha. When it is answered and applied, archive it on GitHub (text plus a decisions.json with every chip answer and note) under `<parasha>/review/archive/`, then move the Google Doc to the trash (review-list-google-doc, "Archiving"). Which docs are open, answered or archived is kept only in review_docs.json in the repo (the registry; review-list-google-doc skill): read it there. Noach Review List 1's book-wide answers: B5 written/read forms (see Hebrew), B6 follow the Masorah spellings, E1 a title page for each parasha, E2 the first verse of a parasha that starts mid-chapter shows its chapter (6:9, [ו] ט), E3 the note on the vowels at the front of the book, E5–E7 (see Page look).
- **A proposal row should say plainly what each answer does.** When a row's Approve could mean either "keep the PDF" or "do what you asked for", ask David before applying (in Noach he meant "do it" for E5–E7, his own requests). For a new list, prefer two clear options per row: "Do it" / "Leave it".
- **Sefaria column.** (David's rule, Oct 2026; shortened the same day to save room.) Every review-table row about Ibn Ezra's text gets a column placed after Alternative and before Decision, and the cell holds only one blue link whose text is the single word "Sefaria". No quotation of Sefaria's English in the cell. The link goes to that exact comment: https://www.sefaria.org/Ibn_Ezra_on_Genesis.<chapter>.<verse>.<comment>?lang=bi. Sefaria sometimes files a comment under a different verse than the printed edition (e.g. the cherubim at 3:23), so pick the comment by its text, not the verse number. A row about the Chumash verse itself gets the same one-word "Sefaria" link, pointing to the pasuk with the Translations panel open (https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.3.8?lang=bi&with=Translations&lang2=en), because there are several translations to compare. Rows with no text to compare (layout, whole-book rules) get "—" or no Sefaria column. Fill the column when the row is created, not afterwards. (The English there is Strickman & Silver; David reads it on Sefaria.)
- **Review sheets in Google Sheets** (Grammar Clarity Review 1, Oct 2026): one column per place, read downwards (labels in column A, frozen); Hebrew without cantillation, meteg or paseq (Sheets shows them as boxes; keep vowels and dagesh); rows that are not book text shaded grey; the proposed text in orange right under the current text, with its decision dropdown under it. Put each new tab first in the sheet and hide superseded tabs, since the sheet opens on the first tab.
- That includes any grammar term or rule you are unsure of. It goes in the grammar Part of the list (see "When unsure, ask in the review" above). It is never settled by guessing.
- Only rows David has answered are applied. Unanswered and Discuss rows are not touched in the book (a Decision cell with a stray character and no chip counts as unanswered, as A23 in Noach). In the Bereshit lists, Approve meant "the PDF is right as it stands" for Parts A–E of Review List 2, but "apply the proposal" for rows that propose a change (Part G, and all of Review List 3); Noach Review List 1 used the Review List 2 meaning (Approve = the PDF is right; Change = use the Alternative). Read the list's own "How to use" before applying. Keep on a suggestion row means "don't do it".
- When a note asks you to check Sefaria, check it and report what Sefaria says before applying.
- (27 Oct 2026) The full procedure for a new rule — rule file, core line, a test in scripts/check_all.py where a program can check it, then the book — is in the core skill, "A new rule from David".
- A rule David states once becomes a standing rule for later parasha only if it is saved in this skill, so when he gives a new general rule, add it here (propose the skill update) in the same turn. For the next parasha, the rules in this skill are built in from the first draft.
- Never overwrite David's Decision or Your note.
- When explaining a row, give where + what, the Hebrew line, and the full sentence as it stands and if changed, each with its English.
- **Before each draft, run `python3 -I scripts/check_all.py <folder>`** (repo top; it runs consonant_check, vowel_check, misprint_scan and check_book, and checks Text lines, Onkelos lines, ה׳ in the English and the page numbers; hits already read are kept in `<folder>/review/check_baseline.json` and only NEW hits are listed; after reading every new hit, rerun with `--accept`). check_book alone: `python3 scripts/check_book.py <model.json> build/rep.json`** (row D1): one report of every check (markup, Hashem, letter and vowel names, stray characters, typing slips, empty Text labels, "agrees with Sefaria", source lines, reference brackets, glosses on defined words, verse-band brackets, term spellings, the terms audit, and the layout report). Read every hit; fix the mechanical ones, make rows of the translation ones ("Heth" and "Shin’ar" are false hits of the letter check; Lech Lecha 15:2 and 15:8 are expected hits of the Hashem check). Also run clean.py --check (see Notes). Also read the front and back matter each time: the preface describes the book's conventions, and when a rule changes the preface must change with it (in draft 8 it still said every note ends with a Text line and that letter names stay in English).
