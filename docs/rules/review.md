# The review process

How a change reaches the book: everything goes to you as a row in a review list, you answer it, and only answered rows are applied. These rules say what a review list contains, how its rows are written, and how answers are applied. (How the Google Doc itself is built is in **09 Review doc**.) Each rule gives the rule, an example and the reason; the numbers ("Ruling 83") are entries in **03 Rulings**. The last part, "For Claude", says how each rule is carried out.

## How a change reaches the book

### 1. What you ask for becomes a review row, not a new draft
**Rule.** When you ask to add or change something — a new rule, a mistake you found, a wording change, an extra note, a layout change — it becomes a row in the open review list, even if you do not say "review", and Claude says in one line which row it is. A change that affects the whole book is one row listing every place; it may go into whichever list is open.

**Except** (then it is done straight away and Claude says so):
- you ask directly to fix the layout or how the PDF looks, or say how a sign or bracket should be printed — fixed, and a new draft sent;
- you give a plain instruction while asking for a draft ("remove these from the notes", "add this as well");
- Claude asks you with checkboxes whether to apply a change now, and you say yes;
- you say to put a change into the parashot and rebuild them (with a review sheet alongside), or to apply and rebuild.

**Example.** The Bereshit and Noach place names went into Noach Review List 2, Part C, as one row each.

**Why.** You see and decide every change before it is in the book. *Ruling 83.*

### 2. A new draft only when you ask for one
**Rule.** A new draft is built only when you ask for a draft, after you have answered rows and said to apply them, or for a layout fix you asked for. The book, its data and the glossary are not changed otherwise.

**Why.** Each draft you see is the result of your own decisions.

### 3. Every open question is a row
**Rule.** Every open question goes to you as a row in the review list — including any grammar term Claude is unsure of (**05 Grammar**, rule 3). It is never settled by guessing.

**Why.** Questions buried in chat get lost; a row has its own answer box.

### 4. One review list per parasha, kept in Drive
**Rule.** Each parasha (or each round of review) gets its own short review list. While it is open or answered it sits in the private Drive folder "-02 Ibn Ezra — Reviews in progress". When it has been applied, it is archived — its text and every answer saved on GitHub — and moved to "-01 Ibn Ezra — Older reviews". Both folders are inside "Ibn Ezra on the Torah - PDFs", with limited access, so the book's public link does not open them. A review doc is never deleted.

**Why.** A long document stops opening on your phone; and you asked for "a section in the Google Drive with all of the current reviews in progress", with the finished ones kept in a folder of their own. *Rulings 84, 85, 86.*

## What every review list contains

### 5. An Ideas Part
**Rule.** Every review list has a Part of its own for Ibn Ezra's ideas, his science and his hints, with one row per idea paragraph, even in a new parasha's draft 1: Now = the note without the paragraph, Alternative = with it, Keep / Approve. An idea paragraph is never final until you have answered its row.

**Example.** Toledot List 1, Part F, as rebuilt in the old style.

**Why.** "Always have them in a section of the review so I can see if I want to keep them." *Rulings 87, 56.*

### 6. Place names and names of people
**Rule.** Every parasha's list has a Place names row (**04 Translation**, rule 13) and the names of people with their other spelling. The names that only confirm the draft are one row for the Part, each name numbered, with where it appears (Now) and its other spelling (the Hebrew form or the other English one). A name you have already confirmed is not asked again: it keeps the confirmed spelling. A name that also stands in earlier parashot changes there only through a row of its own. Names with no second spelling in use are listed in the doc's closing section.

**Example.** Rebekah / Rebecca. Chayei Sarah Review List 1, Part H, was the first model.

**Why.** "For names of people make sure to have a row for their name and their alternative, for example Rebekah and Rebecca"; and "if you have already named them before and I have confirmed it you do not need to ask again about them". *Rulings 89, 90.*

### 7. One row for each proposed change; places that only confirm the draft are bundled
**Rule.** Every row that proposes a change is its own row, with its own Hebrew, English and Decision. Places that only confirm the draft — misprints already corrected in it, new names as it spells them, Avi Ezer remarks it does not take up — go in **one row per Part**, each place numbered. Approve or Keep on that row = every place as in the draft; Change = write in Your note the numbers of the places to change. A place where the list proposes something else stays its own row.

**Example.** In the audits, where rule fixes were made without asking and only judgment calls became rows, you approved 84 of 130 rows (65%); in the parasha lists from Vayera on, 33 of 152 (22%), and the Keeps were misprints one by one (Vayera 16 of 16, Toledot 10 of 10), names (Chayei Sarah 30 of 34) and Avi Ezer remarks (Chayei Sarah 36 of 43) — these are now bundled.

**Why.** "You have condensed things into one block, I want it all expanded" — for the changes; the bundling, after you compared the old reviews with the new ones. *Rulings 91, 92.*

### 8. One English for one Hebrew phrase, across the book
**Rule.** The phrases Ibn Ezra repeats are read at every place in the book. Where the book gives one phrase more than one English, it becomes a row in a Consistency Review, with every place listed. Differences the glossary allows (the modern name at a term's first mention on a page) are not raised. This is run again before each new parasha's draft 1 and after each parasha is final.

**Why.** "Do all of them" — the list of improvements. *Ruling 96.*

## How a row is written

### 9. Every row says why, in plain words
**Rule.** Every row has a plain "why" — a sentence or two — first in its Explanation. The Issue states the point itself and never sends you to another row.

**Example.** "Lot spoke in the singular to the angel he thought the greater; the printed word has a ה where a ח belongs." Not "so Avi Ezer, row D8".

**Why.** For a time the Explanation said only "Keep = as now. Approve = the change", and the rows you could not decide on (Toledot B3, "Still not clear"; Front Matter C5) were the ones without a plain reason. *Ruling 92.*

### 10. The Now box shows the Hebrew it translates — the clause only
**Rule.** Wherever Now and Alternative are two renderings of a Hebrew text (a verse translation, or Ibn Ezra's English), the Now box opens with that Hebrew on its own line, with vowels: only the clause the row is about, not the whole comment. Rows that add or change a note keep the Hebrew in the Explanation.

**Why.** "Make sure that it shows the Hebrew in the box when it is relevant … that way I can read the Hebrew and make the decision on which version." *Rulings 93, 92.*

### 11. An idea row's Alternative is the English paragraph alone
**Rule.** In an idea row, the Alternative is the English paragraph alone. The Hebrew of the source it rests on is in the Explanation, as "Source (Hebrew)".

**Why.** With the source Hebrew in the box, an idea row doubled in length. *Ruling 92.*

### 12. Every Explanation shows the Hebrew and the text both ways
**Rule.** Each row's Explanation gives the Hebrew line (Ibn Ezra's, or the verse when the row has none), then the sentence as it stands and as it would be if changed, each with its English. When Claude explains a row in chat, it does the same: where and what, the Hebrew line, and the full sentence both ways with English.

**Why.** Your standing preference: you decide from the Hebrew and the full sentence. *Ruling 94.*

### 13. Each row says plainly what each answer does
**Rule.** A row says plainly what Approve and Keep each do: **Approve** = do what the Alternative says; **Keep** = leave the book as it is. Where possible a row has two clear options ("Do it" / "Leave it").

**Why.** In Noach, Approve on E5–E7 could mean either "the PDF is right" or "do what I asked"; it had to be asked again.

### 14. A Sefaria link on rows about the text
**Rule.** Every row about Ibn Ezra's text has a column after Alternative with one blue link, the single word "Sefaria", to that exact comment. A row about the Chumash verse links to the verse with the translations panel open. Rows with nothing to compare (layout, whole-book rules) have no link.

**Why.** You read the published translation on Sefaria beside the row. *Ruling 98.*

### 15. A progress line
**Rule.** Under each doc's subtitle: "Progress (date): N of M answered (Approve …, Keep …) · open: …", brought up to date each time the answers are read. It touches no row, chip or note.

**Why.** "Do all of them" — the list of improvements. *Ruling 95.*

## Answers

### 16. Only answered rows are applied
**Rule.** Only rows you have answered are applied. Unanswered and Discuss rows are not touched in the book (a Decision cell with a stray character and no chip counts as unanswered). Keep on a suggestion means "don't do it". What Approve meant has changed between lists, so each list's own "How to use" is read before applying it.

**Example.** In the Bereshit lists, Approve meant "the PDF is right" in Parts A–E of Review List 2, but "apply the proposal" elsewhere; Noach Review List 1 used the first meaning.

**Why.** Nothing goes into the book without your answer.

### 17. Discuss rows are explained, then asked with checkboxes
**Rule.** A Discuss row is explained in plain words — the Hebrew, what each choice means, a real example — and the decision is asked with checkboxes in the same turn. Where a Change note can be read two ways, Claude asks with checkboxes before applying. Your answer in chat counts as your answer; your chip in the doc is never changed.

**Example.** Front Matter C5: "I want to approve but I don't understand what you mean by the 'Rule: the first place…'".

**Why.** A Discuss row means the row was not clear enough to answer. *Ruling 97.*

### 18. Your Decision and Your note are never overwritten
**Rule.** Claude never writes over your Decision or Your note, in any doc.

**Why.** They are your words.

### 19. Sefaria is checked when you ask, before applying
**Rule.** When a note or a row asks Claude to check Sefaria, it checks before applying and tells you what Sefaria shows.

**Example.** Lech Lecha 17:10 and 13:7.

**Why.** "Confirm with Sefaria." *Ruling 99.*

### 20. A row that has to be done by hand is never skipped
**Rule.** An approved row that the program cannot apply by itself stops the applying until it has been done by hand. A names row is applied by the program, and your answer goes into the list of confirmed names.

**Why.** "Do all of them" — the list of improvements. *Ruling 100.*

## Before each draft, and older parashot

### 21. Every check is run before a draft
**Rule.** Before each draft every check of the parasha is run and every new finding read: mechanical slips are fixed, translation questions become rows. When a rule changes, the front and back matter are read too, since the preface describes the book's conventions.

**Example.** Draft 8 of Bereshit still said in the preface that every note ends with a Text line and that letter names stay in English.

**Why.** Nothing is sent that the checks would have caught.

### 22. An older parasha is brought up to new rules by an audit
**Rule.** When the rules change, a parasha already written is brought up to them by an audit: pure rule fixes are applied with a log, and every judgment call goes to you in one review doc per parasha. The pages are renumbered once, at the end.

**Why.** The audit of Bereshit to Chayei Sarah (Audits 1–6) was done this way.

### 23. A rule you state once becomes a standing rule
**Rule.** When you state a rule, it is written into these rules in the same turn, with a ruling in **03 Rulings**, so it holds from the first draft of every later parasha. (The steps are in **12 Core skill**, "A new rule from David".)

**Why.** A rule said once in chat would otherwise be forgotten in the next session.

## For Claude: how it is done and checked

- **Rules 1–3:** a row has where, the Hebrew line, Now, Proposal / Alternative, Decision chip, Your note, and an Explanations-tab entry with links (09 Review doc). The review-list-google-doc skill builds the doc.
- **Rule 4:** `refresh_drive()` → `sync_reviews()` (scripts/workbench/wb.py) moves open and answered docs into "Reviews in progress" (id 1O7WtvLyYhia5hb0PldzL5NKzgZMLRStN) and archives every doc set to `applied` (text + decisions.json committed under `<parasha>/review/archive/`), then moves it to "Older reviews" (id 1jUeiNkiIifdx6nSU8U8tosrQOjf5LX9A). Which docs are open, answered or archived is kept only in review_docs.json (the registry). Details: review_doc.md, "The reviews folder on Drive" and "Archiving".
- **Rule 5:** ibn-ezra-idea-notes; rows that fill the same place are anchored on the sentence after it, so approved rows go in in ID order. Each row names its source, read on Sefaria, and is checked by cite_check (notes.md, rule 13).
- **Rule 6:** `docs/rules/confirmed_names.json` (name → the row that settled it); add to it whenever David answers a names row. review_audit/make_rows.py stops on a names row for a confirmed name.
- **Rule 7:** `bundle=[{where, now, heb, en, why, name}]` in the rows file: the places numbered in Now, each with its Hebrew and English in the Explanation. apply_answers needs nothing on Approve or Keep (a bundled names row puts every name into confirmed_names.json) and stops on Change for the numbered places, to be made by hand.
- **Rule 8:** review_audit/consistency/ (tasks.json: the phrases Ibn Ezra repeats; renderings.json: the book's English at each place) → consistency1_rows.py.
- **Rules 9–11:** review_audit/make_rows.py does them (for every op on `verses.en` or `ec`): it takes the clauses of Ibn Ezra's Hebrew that contain the words the English excerpt quotes (by position only when it quotes none; the whole comment only under 120 letters); `heb_box` (a marker such as `{hcw:c:v:i-j}`) overrides it — Toledot B2 and B4 are the models — and make_rows warns where it found none. It puts the source passage (citations.json) at the head of an idea row's "If changed" as "Source (Hebrew)", right to left. It stops a new list on a missing `why` or an Issue that points to another row (lists made earlier only warn; the list is LEGACY in make_rows).
- **Rule 12:** a row with no ops (made by hand) must give `changed` — the text as it would read — and `changed_he` where Hebrew changes; make_rows prints EXPLANATION WARNINGS otherwise, fixed before the doc is built. In chat: `python3 -I scripts/explain_rows.py <review> <IDs | --discuss | --change | --open | --noted>` (explain-commentary-row).
- **Rule 14:** the link text is the single word "Sefaria", no quotation in the cell. Comment: https://www.sefaria.org/Ibn_Ezra_on_Genesis.<chapter>.<verse>.<comment>?lang=bi — Sefaria sometimes files a comment under another verse than the printed edition (the cherubim at 3:23), so pick the comment by its text. Verse: https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.3.8?lang=bi&with=Translations&lang2=en. Rows with nothing to compare: "—" or no column. Fill it when the row is made.
- **Rule 15:** made by the Builder for a new doc; read_answers brings it up to date.
- **Rule 16:** read the answers with `read_answers('<key>')` or `review_status()` in the workbench, never by loading the whole doc.
- **Rule 17:** save the chat answer to `<rows file>` → chat_answers.json ({id: {decision, note, date}}); apply-review-decisions reads it after answers.json.
- **Rule 20:** review_audit/apply_answers.py applies a names row itself (Approve changes the name in the parasha's work files; either answer goes into confirmed_names.json; a name also found in another parasha is reported for a book-wide row) and stops on any other approved row with no ops until it is made by hand and listed in `<key>_by_hand.json`.
- **Rule 21:** `python3 -I scripts/check_all.py <folder>` (repo top) runs consonant_check, vowel_check, misprint_scan and check_book, and checks Text lines, Onkelos lines, ה׳ in the English, Hebrew sources, partly pointed words, ideas and idea sources, "its parallel" and the page numbers. Hits already read are kept in `<folder>/review/check_baseline.json`; only NEW hits are listed; after reading every one, rerun with `--accept`. check_book alone: `python3 scripts/check_book.py <model.json> build/rep.json` — one report of every check (markup, Hashem, letter and vowel names, stray characters, typing slips, empty Text labels, "agrees with Sefaria", source lines, reference brackets, glosses on defined words, verse-band brackets, term spellings, the terms audit, the layout report). Fix the mechanical hits, make rows of the translation ones ("Heth" and "Shin’ar" are false hits of the letter check; Lech Lecha 15:2 and 15:8 are expected hits of the Hashem check). Also run clean.py --check (notes.md, "For Claude").
- **Rule 22:** the commentary-audit skill (Parts A–F, renumber once at the end); checklist in review_audit/checklist.md.
- **Review sheets in Google Sheets** (Grammar Clarity Review 1 was one): one column per place, read downwards (labels in column A, frozen); Hebrew without cantillation, meteg or paseq (Sheets shows them as boxes; keep vowels and dagesh); rows that are not book text shaded grey; the proposed text in orange right under the current text, with its decision dropdown under it. Put each new tab first and hide superseded tabs, since the sheet opens on the first tab.
- **A stopped doc build resumes:** build with `Builder.run(cfg)`, which records each finished phase in /mnt/files/review_builds/ and, called again, finishes the same doc instead of making a second one (Ruling 101).
