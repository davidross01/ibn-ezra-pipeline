# House rules — Notes and other commentators

Part of commentary-house-rules (the core is the skill itself). Moved here word for word on 27 Oct 2026; change a rule here and in the core line that points to it.

## Notes and other commentators
- **Onkelos: a main commentator, on par with Rashi** (David, 27 Oct 2026). Add a line wherever Onkelos's rendering bears on Ibn Ezra's reading — where he agrees with it, or renders the word another way (and where Ibn Ezra cites "the Translator"). Quote the key Aramaic words, then translate them yourself. Text: Sefaria, "Targum Onkelos, vocalized according to the Yemenite Taj" (CC-BY-SA; credited in "How this edition was made"), saved as `<parasha>/src/onkelos.json`. Write the lines as data in `<parasha>/work/onkelos.py` (A = add, S = change wording; `{o:i-j}` prints words i–j of the verse's Onkelos by program, never retyped), applied by `scripts/onk_lib.py` from `work/assemble.py` (Bereshit: `bereshit/work/apply_onkelos.py`). The Targum's יְיָ prints as ה׳; do not end a quotation on a prefixed form of the Name (דַּייָ, לַייָ); say "of Hashem" in the English instead. Never write "with the Name" (check_book flags it): write "renders it as ה׳". Order: the Onkelos line goes before Rashi, unless the Rashi line begins "Rashi, too" and refers back to Ibn Ezra; then put Onkelos after it.
- The notes compare Rashi throughout and name Talmud/Midrash sources when Rashi or Ibn Ezra rests on them (checked on Sefaria, but the note itself never says so, see below).
- **Also compare other commentators** (David, row A6, Oct 2026): add a short line for Radak where he discusses Ibn Ezra (by name or his view) or explains him notably, checked on Sefaria. Say exactly who is quoted (e.g. Radak quoting R. Moses ibn Ezra, or an editor's attribution in brackets), never more than the text says.
- **Rashbam: like Rashi, wherever useful** (David, Oct 2026). Add a short line for Rashbam where his plain sense agrees with Ibn Ezra or reads the verse differently, checked on Sefaria (Rashbam_on_Genesis, Hebrew; translate it yourself). On Sefaria his commentary on Genesis has only 1:1–1:3 in Bereshit, and in Noach only 6:9, 8:15, 10:9, 10:15 and 11:4; check Sefaria for each later parasha before saying where it survives. Bereshit has lines at 1:1, 1:2 (twice) and 1:3; Noach uses 6:9 and 11:4. Quote his Hebrew only where his text is pointed; otherwise give the sense in English.
- **Ramban only where he quotes or adds to Ibn Ezra's view** (David, Oct 2026). Keep a Ramban line when he cites Ibn Ezra's explanation, agrees with it, or builds on it (Bereshit kept 1:5, 2:7, 3:8, 6:4). Leave out his objections to Ibn Ezra and his own different readings (Bereshit removed 1:4, 1:5, 1:18, 2:3, 2:19, 2:20, 3:16, 4:7, 4:8, 4:23), and a line where Ramban reaches a reading Ibn Ezra lists without naming him (Noach 8:11 removed, Review List 2 row A2). If one of those seems very relevant, ask David before adding it.
- The preface lists the commentators: "Onkelos and Rashi throughout, Rashbam where his commentary survives (in Bereshit, 1:1–3), Radak where he quotes or answers Ibn Ezra, and Ramban where he quotes or adds to Ibn Ezra's view". Keep it true when the rules change.
- **Grammar notes explain the point, not only state it** (David, Grammar Clarity Review 1, 25 Oct 2026: "at the moment I read it and just think to myself: okay, so what?"). Every note on a grammar point Ibn Ezra makes says, in plain words and inside the note itself, in this order: (1) the **plain point** — what Ibn Ezra shows about the word (root, conjugation, letter, vowel, stress, gender); (2) **"A reader might think …"** — the wrong reading the point prevents (וָאֹכֵל taken for the participle, "and I am eating"; וַיֵּרָא read as וַיַּרְא, "Hashem saw Abram"); where this is Claude's inference, phrase it as a reader's possible misreading, never as Ibn Ezra's own words; (3) the **proof** and **what it does for the word — why he brings it**: what it changes in the meaning, the tense, who acts, the gender, the link to another verse, or only how the verse is read aloud (say so plainly when that is all). Where the "why" is Claude's inference, say "it seems". The grammar sentences may be lightly reworded (David: "allow light rewording of the grammar sentences only and label a reader might think"); everything else in the note stays. **Add to the note; never take away** its other points (Rashi, Radak, Rashbam and Ramban lines, Text lines, other comments on the verse): write the new sentences as insertions or span-replacements and check by program that every Hebrew word and number of the old note is still there (review_grammar/all/apply_additions.py, apply_rewrite.py). Where an addition is weak, leave it out (13 were removed). Applies from draft 1 of every parasha; Bereshit, Noach and Lech Lecha were brought into line in Grammar Clarity Review 1 (Google Sheet 1REUa8uFRAhMWYL72AhAhkatj4_TBWLjZncN_yGKWZ-M, one tab per parasha; David left every addition as drafted). David does not have to review every grammar note; he marks Change or Remove addition only where he disagrees.
- **Notes are part of the book**: a note never mentions the review list ("this is a row in the review list") or says something "has not been checked". Check it, or leave it out and ask in the review.
- **No "Uncertain." labels** (David, 25 Oct 2026): remove the doubt entirely and state the likeliest reading. Earlier rule (Noach B2, C4: drop the label on Approve, keep it on Keep) is superseded. Every passage taken out is recorded in the Editorial record.
- **Sefaria checks: no remarks in the book** (David, Oct 2026, widened 25 Oct 2026). A note never says that a quotation "agrees with Sefaria's text", "(checked on Sefaria)", "Sefaria's text" or "Sefaria's comment has no headword", and makes no remark about the printed headword or the heading of a comment. Say "the Masoretic text" where a text is meant. A note says something only when the quotation differs from the Masoretic text or the printed text has a misprint, in a short {i}Text.{/i} line. If nothing differs, there is no Text line. Sefaria is named only in the credits in the front matter (its licence requires it); the preface says once how headings are chosen.
- **Before each draft run `python3 -I review_grammar/all/clean.py --check`** (repo top): it lists any new "Uncertain", Sefaria or headword remarks. Clean them out the same way (clean.py), add them to review_grammar/all/editorial_record.json and refresh the Editorial record Google Doc (davross846).
- **When David asks to double-check a row on Sefaria, check it before applying and say what Sefaria shows** (Lech Lecha 17:10 and 13:7, 25 Oct 2026).
- **No per-section source lines** (David, Oct 2026). The English commentary does not end each section with "Sefaria: Genesis 1:1-5 (Miqra according to the Masorah); Ibn Ezra on Genesis 1:1-5 (Piotrkow, 1907–1911)". The sources are credited once, in the preface (the Masorah text is CC BY-SA, so the credit must stay there).
- **Ibn Ezra's ideas get a full paragraph in the note** (David, 30 Oct 2026: “when the commentator talks about a philosophical idea, for example Parasha Vayera 18:21 talks about the knowledge of God, make the notes focus on them and explain the concept in detail and why the commentator wants to bring this point and what you would have thought otherwise. This should be done for every parasha, and you can make it part of a review list that I can look over”; his answers the same day: philosophy and theology, his science, and his “secrets” too; where he hints and stops, explain what it points to and name the source; one full paragraph; reviewed as a Part of each parasha's audit doc). Wherever Ibn Ezra discusses an **idea** (God's knowledge, will and names; creation; the angels; the soul; prophecy; providence; the image of God), his **science** (the spheres, astronomy, astrology, the elements) or a **“secret”** (“the wise will understand”, “I cannot explain”, “there is a secret”), the note gives one full paragraph of about 120–200 words, after the plain explanation of the comment and before the other commentators: (1) **the idea**, explained in detail, with the background a reader needs (e.g. the medieval picture of the spheres); (2) **why Ibn Ezra brings it here** — what in the verse, or in another view, prompts it; (3) **what a reader would otherwise have thought**. Where he hints and stops, say what the hint points to and **name the source**: his own comment elsewhere (Exodus 23:21: “He is all, and from Him is all”), or a supercommentary on Sefaria (Avi Ezer) — read on Sefaria before it is cited; never Mechokekei Yehudah (Yahel Ohr, Karnei Ohr), which is Krinsky's. Ramban's objections stay out, as for every Ramban line, unless David asks. Where the reasoning is Claude's own, say “it seems”. From draft 1 of every new parasha; in the parashot already written, as rows in Part F of the audit doc. `scripts/ideas_scan.py <folder>` lists the comments that touch an idea; check_all runs it as “Ideas”, and `<folder>/review/ideas.json` records the verses done or judged not to need a paragraph. (The order, the length and where the paragraph goes are superseded by the rule of 8 Nov 2026 below: fixed order, 120–250 words, the draft without the additions and one row per added sentence.)
- **Every idea paragraph goes to David as a review row** (David, 30 Oct 2026): it is never final until he has answered its row in the list's Ideas Part (review.md).
- **Idea notes: length and sources** (David's answers to Audit 1 and his choices of 30 Oct 2026). **Full** paragraph, nothing cut, for real ideas (God's knowledge and will, the soul, prophecy, providence, creation, the image of God, free will) — when revising one, add, never cut (F1: “don’t cut it out”). **Short**, 3–4 sentences, for background (the spheres, the three worlds, the elements: F3, F5 “shorten”). “Why he brings it here” only where the verse itself prompts the idea (F4: “remove the part about why he brings it here”). **Avi Ezer** (“it's good when you use Avi Ezer to explain in notes”): read on Sefaria for every idea and every hint, and also wherever it notably explains Ibn Ezra's plain sense or grammar, like Radak; named in the note. **Rambam**: wherever Ibn Ezra's idea is one Rambam discusses (Guide of the Perplexed, Mishneh Torah), the note gives Rambam's view, read on Sefaria (F1: “add in the whole element of free will … Rambam”). Aristotle is not brought in (F1: “drop Aristotle”).
- **Idea notes: a fixed order, Ibn Ezra first, Saadia, and a limit** (David, 8 Nov 2026: “I want you to do with every parasha 1. Give each idea paragraph a fixed order 2. Bring in Saadia on the same idea, not just the same verse. 3. Use Ibn Ezra to explain Ibn Ezra first. 4. Keep a limit so the notes don’t swell … Write it into the house rules as Saadia on the same idea, the treatise and chapter named and read on Sefaria, and Ibn Ezra’s own writings (Yesod Mora and his other comments). add it to the review”). Every idea paragraph keeps one order: (1) **what Ibn Ezra says**, the idea explained; (2) **what a reader would otherwise think**; (3) **Ibn Ezra explained by Ibn Ezra** — his own writings first: *Yesod Mora* and his comments on other verses and books (Torah, Isaiah, Psalms, Job, Ecclesiastes, Daniel …), wherever he says more on the same idea, and always where he says “as I will explain” or “as I explained”; Avi Ezer where he explains Ibn Ezra's words; (4) **Saadia Gaon** — *Emunot veDeot* (on Sefaria as *HaEmunot veHaDeot*, Ibn Tibbon's Hebrew), wherever Saadia discusses the **same idea**, not merely the same verse, with the treatise and chapter named (“Emunot veDeot III:1”) and read on Sefaria; especially where Ibn Ezra follows him, answers him or calls him “the Gaon”; (5) **Rambam after him**. **Limit:** Ibn Ezra's own writings and Saadia one or two sentences each; the paragraph stays a paragraph (about 120–250 words with them); background stays 3–4 sentences, with Saadia only if he speaks to it directly. Where Emunot veDeot has nothing on the idea, nothing is said in the note. His choices the same day: in the parashot already written, the paragraphs are **not rewritten** — the new sentences are **added** in their place in the order, each as its own row (“add sentences only”); one review doc per parasha (“Ideas Audit — <Parasha>”), and an open list's Ideas Part is updated in place; places he kept (Keep) or that were judged “not needed” are raised again where Ibn Ezra's own writings or Saadia add something. `<folder>/review/idea_sources.json` records, for every place in ideas.json, what was read: {"c:v": {"ibn_ezra": "Yesod Mora 5:1 (row …)" | "nothing further", "saadia": "III:1 (row …)" | "nothing on this idea"}}; check_all lists the places not yet recorded as “Idea sources” (like Ideas, never remembered as read).
