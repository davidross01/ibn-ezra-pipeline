# Hebrew, misprints and variants

The rules for the Hebrew on the page — its letters, its vowels, Hebrew inside the English — and for misprints and variant readings. Each rule gives the rule, an example and the reason; the numbers ("Ruling 31") are entries in **03 Rulings**. The last part, "For Claude", says how each rule is carried out and checked.

## The letters

### 1. The source's letters are never changed, and Hebrew is never typed from memory
**Rule.** Only vowelled sources are used. No Hebrew letter of the source is changed, and no Hebrew is retyped from memory: a change is made inside the existing text, and passages are matched by their consonants (vowels and cantillation stripped). A letter lost in the source's text layer is restored only when a second witness, such as Sefaria, has the same letter.

**Why.** A Hebrew word typed from memory can be wrong by a letter or a vowel without anyone noticing; copying keeps the text exactly as its source.

### 2. The Hebrew commentary has Sefaria's letters, letter for letter
**Rule.** From Noach on, the letters of Ibn Ezra's Hebrew are Sefaria's, letter for letter (the note on the vowels promises it). When adding vowels, a word is never shortened or lengthened: where Ibn Ezra spells a word fully, his ו or י stays and the vowel moves onto it. A letter is never changed to the Bible's spelling or to a "better" word. Only a misprint correction in [square brackets] may add letters (rule 11).

**Example.** תואר, גופר, ביאר become תּוֹאַר, גוֹפֶר, בֵּיאֵר — the full spelling kept. Noach draft 1 at first had silently made דעת into דעה, ומת into וימת, החת into החתי; all three were put back.

**Why.** The reader can trust that the Hebrew is the text as printed, with nothing improved silently.

### 3. Ibn Ezra's own spelling in the headings
**Rule.** Where Ibn Ezra's lemma is spelled differently from the verse, the English-side heading of the comment and of the note uses his spelling, as his Hebrew commentary does. The verse band keeps the verse's spelling. No Text line is needed.

**Example.** Noach 10:11–12: הַהִיא in the headings, הַהִוא in the verse.

**Why.** "Keep the Ibn Ezra's." The heading quotes him, so it is spelled as he spells it. *Ruling 30.*

### 4. Written and read forms (ketiv and qere) in the verse band
**Rule.** Where a word is written one way and read another, the verse band prints the written form without vowels and without brackets, then the reading, with vowels, in square brackets. The note gives the written form and says which one Ibn Ezra comments on.

**Example.** Noach 8:17: הוצא [הַיְצֵא]. Bereshit has none; Noach only this one.

**Why.** "Always do this for when it is written and said in a different way, but just make the written version without brackets and without vowels." *Ruling 31.*

### 5. The verse band follows the Masorah's spelling
**Rule.** The verse band follows Miqra according to the Masorah, even where common printed Chumashim spell a word differently.

**Example.** Noach 7:11 מַעְיְנֹת, 9:29 וַיִּהְיוּ.

**Why.** One standard text for the whole book. *Ruling 33.*

## The vowels

### 6. Every word is pointed in full; Sefaria's vowels are a guide
**Rule.** Every Hebrew word gets its full vowels. Sefaria's vowels are a reference, still checked: where Sefaria prints a word with partial or wrong vowels, the book completes or corrects them (the verse's vowels for a quotation, standard grammar otherwise). A word Sefaria already prints with vowels inside Ibn Ezra keeps them where they are right — lemmas and quotations included — with no Text line.

**Example.** וְנָבֵלה and וְנָסֵבָה at Noach 11:7, and וַתַּקח, וַתִּשְלְכִי at Lech Lecha 12:15, keep Sefaria's marks.

**Why.** "Fill in all of the vowels correctly, Sefaria's vowels are to be a reference and still check if they are correct." *Rulings 32, 37.*

### 7. Quotations of Scripture take the Bible's vowels
**Rule.** A quotation of Scripture, and a lemma, takes the vowels of Miqra according to the Masorah — without cantillation or meteg, but keeping the dagesh it has in its context, its kamatz katan and its pausal forms — carried over to Ibn Ezra's spelling. Abbreviations, the ד' of the Hebrew commentary, letter names, and book names and numbers in references stay without vowels.

**Example.** וַיָּמׇת, קׇדָשִׁים, פַּלְגֵי מָיִם. Unpointed: בי"ת, הנון, כיו"ד.

**Why.** A quotation should look exactly like the verse it quotes.

### 8. The vowels of Ibn Ezra's own words are ours, and the book says so
**Rule.** The vowels of Ibn Ezra's own words were added by Claude and are not checked against a vowelled edition. The preface and the note on the vowels say so, and that statement is kept true.

**Why.** You chose to keep it so after a sample check, rather than check every word against a printed edition. *Ruling 34.*

### 9. The abbreviation פי' is written out
**Rule.** פי' is written out in full: פֵּירוּשׁ (with its prefix: וּפֵירוּשׁ, בְּפֵירוּשׁ, הַפֵּירוּשׁ, שֶׁפֵּירוּשׁ), or the verb פֵּירֵשׁ / לְפָרֵשׁ where the English reads "explains" or "to explain". It is the one allowed difference from Sefaria's letters. Other abbreviations stay as printed and without vowels. The note on the vowels and "How this edition was made" say so.

**Why.** "Expand them so it says the whole word." *Ruling 38.*

## Hebrew inside the English

### 10. Hebrew in the English commentary and the notes is copied, never typed
**Rule.** Every Hebrew word in the English commentary and the notes is copied by program from its source — the verse or one near it, Ibn Ezra, a commentator, Onkelos, the whole Tanakh or the Talmud — and never typed. A grammar example made up for a note (a form not found in Scripture) is replaced by a real one with its verse, unless the note says it is hypothetical ("a reader might expect …").

**Why.** A typed word can carry a wrong letter or vowel; a copied one traces back to its source. *Rulings 35, 36.*

## Misprints and variants

### 11. A misprint is shown as (printed) [correction]
**Rule.** A misprinted word is shown in the Hebrew as the printed form in round brackets and the correction, with vowels, in square brackets. The English follows the correction unless you decide otherwise, and every correction gets a short *Text.* line in the note on the same verse. Where you choose to leave a misprint uncorrected, the Hebrew shows only the printed text and there is no Text line.

**Example.** Bereshit 2:19 (כיאור) [בֵּיאוּר], with its *Text.* line in the note. (Lech Lecha 12:11 יהי' was first left as printed by your choice; you then approved its correction in Proofreading Audit 1.)

**Why.** The reader sees both what was printed and what is meant. *Rulings 40, 41.*

### 12. A wrong verse reference is corrected inside its brackets
**Rule.** The printed reference stays, and the right one follows it in square brackets, all inside the source's one pair of round brackets.

**Example.** (תהלים קמח ה [קמח ח]), not (תהלים קמח ה) [קמח ח].

**Why.** The correction stays visibly part of the same reference. *Ruling 42.*

### 13. Variant readings are your choice
**Rule.** Edition brackets and variant readings (צ"ל, נ"א, manuscripts) are a translation choice for you, never a silent fix. An emendation that is more than a one-letter slip is marked as such in its review row.

**Example.** Noach 10:21 אבי הם → אָבִיו חָם.

**Why.** Choosing a reading changes what Ibn Ezra says.

## Showing Hebrew to you

### 14. Hebrew with its vowels, no transliteration
**Rule.** When Claude shows you Hebrew — in chat or in a review — it shows the Hebrew itself, with vowels, and no transliteration.

**Why.** Your standing preference. *Ruling 29.*

## Bereshit's text

### 15. Every change to Bereshit goes through its change list
**Rule.** Every change to Bereshit after the Onkelos lines is one entry in a single list of changes, and the text is rebuilt from it. The text file is never edited by hand.

**Why.** Bereshit was built differently from the other parashot; the list keeps every change traceable to its review row. *Ruling 39.*

## For Claude: how it is done and checked

- **Rule 2:** before every draft, `<folder>/work/consonant_check.py` strips vowels, markup and [corrections] and compares every word with Sefaria; it must print "differences: 0". `work/fix_letters.py` restores Sefaria's ו/י automatically where only a vowel letter differs and lists the rest for hand fixing. consonant_check counts פי' and its written-out forms as one word (rule 9).
- **Rule 4:** Sefaria's MAM marks written/read forms with `mam-kq` spans; check every parasha for them.
- **Rules 6–7:** `work/vowel_check.py` checks lemmas against the verse and its neighbours and quotations against their references; read every hit (many are look-alike words, not errors). `scripts/partial_vowels.py` lists partly pointed words; check_all runs it as "Partly pointed".
- **Rule 9:** the pointed forms are copied from the book's own Hebrew (review_round/apply_vowels1.py did the four parashot written then).
- **Rule 10:** every {h}/{hb}/{w} span must trace by consonants to its source: the verse or one near it, Ibn Ezra, a commentator's src file, Onkelos, src/tanakh.json (all 39 books, Miqra according to the Masorah) or src/talmud.json. `scripts/source_check.py <folder>` checks it; check_all runs it as "Hebrew sources" (not found, or found only far from the verse; read every hit, `--accept` only the right ones). From Chayei Sarah on, Scripture in a note or comment is written `{q:Book ch:v:i-j}` (words i..j of that verse, Masorah vowels, no cantillation or meteg, the Name as ה׳; `python3 -I scripts/onk_lib.py words "Isaiah 1:1"` numbers the words) and Onkelos `{o:i-j}`; work/assemble.py resolves both. Other markers: `{ie:ch:v:i-j}`, `{iep:ch:v:n}`, `{vow:name}`. The first hits went to "Ibn Ezra — Hebrew Sources Review 1" (review_sources/).
- **Rule 11:** the Text line is {i}Text.{/i}. Misprints are found with the commentary-misprint-finder skill (`scripts/misprint_scan.py`).
- **Rule 15:** `bereshit/work/changes.py` holds every change as `C(row, sec, (ch, v), old, new)`; `python3 -I bereshit/work/build_model.py` rebuilds data/model_final5.json from data/model_final5_pre_onkelos.json + Onkelos + the change file (each entry applied exactly once; log in bereshit/review/changes_log.json). Never edit model_final5.json by hand. The other parashot change in work/chN.py (review_round/subs.py), then work/assemble.py.
