# Page look

How a page of the book looks (the Seforno-volume template), where things go on it, the front and back matter, and the page numbers through the whole book. Each rule gives the rule, an example and the reason; the numbers ("Ruling 61") are entries in **03 Rulings**. The last part, "For Claude", gives the exact settings and checks.

## The page

### 1. What is on a page, in order
**Rule.** On top, the verse band: the Hebrew Chumash in its usual look (vowels, bold, cantillation) with the English beside it. Under it: the Hebrew commentary (right to left), a divider, the English commentary, a rule, the notes in two columns, and at the foot a strip that defines the grammar terms used on that page. No rishon labels and no boxed aliyah marks. Ibn Ezra's Hebrew commentary is set a little bigger than it was at first, even where that moves a comment to the next page.

**Why.** It is the layout of the Seforno volume you chose as the model; the Hebrew is bigger because the pages had room at the bottom. *Ruling 111.*

### 2. The verse band
**Rule.** The Chumash Hebrew is a little smaller than at first. The English and the Hebrew start on the same line. A new chapter starts a new paragraph, in both the Hebrew and the English, in every parasha, Bereshit included.

**Why.** The two columns read side by side from the first line, and a new chapter is easy to see. *Rulings 61, 62, 64.*

### 3. Section marks
**Rule.** After a verse that ends a section of the Torah scroll, the verse band prints the letter: פ for an "open portion" (פָּרָשָׁה פְּתוּחָה: the text ends and the new section begins on a fresh line) and ס for a "closed portion" (פָּרָשָׁה סְתוּמָה: a gap in the middle of a line). The mark is small and bold after the verse, and both columns start a new paragraph there. Each parasha ends with פ פ פ. The preface ("How to read a page") explains both letters.

**Why.** The reader sees the sections as they stand in a Torah scroll. *Ruling 63.*

### 4. Comments go on the page of their verse
**Rule.** Each page gets its verses and then **all** the Hebrew and English comments on those verses; the notes fill what is left. Only when one verse's comments are longer than a page do they continue overleaf, Hebrew and English advancing together. If a page would otherwise be left more than about 15% empty, the next verse starts at the foot of the page with the start of its comments, continuing overleaf. A parasha never ends on a page of leftover notes. Nothing may run into a divider.

**Example.** Only Bereshit 1:1 and 1:26 have comments longer than a page.

**Why.** "It makes sense that it should be there as the verse is on that page." The 15% is not lowered to fill a few more pages: that split six more verses' comments across pages, and you said of short pages, "you don't need to go crazy". *Rulings 67, 68.*

### 5. Notes stay with their verse's page
**Rule.** The notes stay on their verse's page wherever they fit and run on to the next page only when they don't. A few pages then end with some white space, which is accepted. The notes have no chapter numbers (the English commentary keeps them). In every parasha, Bereshit included.

**Why.** A note is read beside its verse. *Rulings 65, 66.*

### 6. Lemmas in bold
**Rule.** The lemma — the word of the verse a comment opens with — is bold. For emphasis in a grammar example, the English word that carries the point is enlarged, not the Hebrew.

**Why.** As in the Seforno volume: the reader finds the word being explained at a glance.

### 7. The foot-of-page definitions are very simple
**Rule.** Each term in the strip at the foot of the page gets a plain, clear definition that anyone can understand, with a short everyday example where it helps, and no technical words inside a definition. Vowels show their sign on an alef (אָ), the dagesh in a ב (בּ). The strip takes up to three lines (four, slightly smaller, on a crowded page), so that every term used on the page is listed. If a term does not fit, a definition is shortened rather than the term dropped.

**Example.** "construct — a word joined to the next one by 'of': 'house of the king'". On Bereshit page 8, "heavy conjugation" became just "Ibn Ezra's name for the pi'el" so that "suffix" fits.

**Why.** The strip is for a reader who does not know grammar. *Ruling 69.*

## Parashot and the whole book

### 8. Each parasha opens with its own title page
**Rule.** A parasha inside the book opens with a title page, like Bereshit's half-title: its Hebrew name, a short rule, and its English name. A parasha that starts in the middle of a chapter shows the chapter with its first verse.

**Example.** פָּרָשַׁת נֹחַ — Parashat Noach; the first verse shows 6:9 / [ו] ט.

**Why.** "I want the title to have a page of its own." *Ruling 73.*

### 9. One book, page numbers carried on
**Rule.** The whole Chumash is one book, built a parasha at a time. Each parasha starts on the page after the last page of the one before; the numbering never restarts at 1. When a parasha's page count changes, every later parasha and its Key Insights booklet are rebuilt (the booklet cites page numbers). The front matter appears once, at the start of the book. Later the book may be split into volumes by size — as the Seforno edition does (Bereshit with Shemot, then the other three), or five, one per book — you decide that later, so each book's pages are kept easy to split off.

**Why.** It is planned as one Chumash. *Ruling 74.*

### 10. The front matter, at the start of the book only
**Rule.** The front matter (pages i–x) is: title page, contents, preface, "How this edition was made", the key to terms (◆ where Ibn Ezra differs from modern grammar), then the note on the vowels. The note on the vowels is at the beginning of the book only, never at the end of a parasha. Each key-to-terms entry ends with "First:" and its first place in each parasha.

**Why.** "The notes on the vowels should be put at the beginning of the book only and not at the end of any parasha, including Bereshit." The "First:" places let a reader find each term in use. *Rulings 70, 71.*

### 11. A glossary at the back of the book
**Rule.** The back of the full book has a glossary: every term of the foot-of-page strip in the final parashot, once each, A–Z, with the same plain definition as in the strip, in a two-column table (term, definition), numbered on from the last page. The Contents lists it after the parashot, and the preface's "Terms at the foot of the page" says it is there. The strip stays on the pages, and the key to terms stays as it is. A term added to a parasha reaches the glossary when that parasha is final. The glossary is never edited by hand.

**Why.** "Add a section which will be at the back of the book which has all of the definitions of words which a reader might be confused on which are currently at the very bottom of the page … it should be in alphabetical order." *Ruling 72.*

### 12. Front and back pages end above the foot
**Rule.** The text of a front or back page never runs below the foot of the page (these pages have no strip). A build with such a page is not sent.

**Example.** The key to terms on page vii once ran off the page (nif'al, hitpa'el and "whose agent is not named" were cut, the "First:" places having grown with each parasha); they now open page viii under "CONJUGATIONS, CONTINUED".

**Why.** A line below the foot is lost in print.

### 13. Licensing and print set-up wait for you
**Rule.** The credits, trim size, margins and font embedding are not changed until you decide on licensing and printing.

**Why.** "Decide at a later date." *Ruling 75.*

## How the pages are built

### 14. One browser version per parasha
**Rule.** Each parasha is laid out with one version of the browser that paginates it, from its draft 1 to its final, and an older parasha is rebuilt only where its version is available — otherwise you are asked before it is re-paginated.

**Example.** Noach, laid out with Chromium 141, rebuilds at 38 pages instead of 39 with Chromium 153. Since the audit rebuild every parasha is on 153.

**Why.** Page breaks depend on the browser; one version keeps rebuilds identical. *Ruling 76.*

### 15. One shared set of build scripts
**Rule.** From Noach on, every parasha is built with the same shared scripts, fonts and hyphenation; Bereshit keeps its own, since it opens the volume. A change to the shared scripts changes every parasha's next build, so it is made only through an answered review row, and every parasha it touches is rebuilt.

**Why.** "Do all of them" — the list of improvements. Before the switch every parasha was built both ways and came out the same, page for page. *Ruling 77.*

## For Claude: how it is done and checked

- **Rule 2:** verse-band Hebrew 19.6 px on 28 px lines (was 21.2/30). book.css `.band { align-items: first baseline; }` aligns the columns on their first baseline (before, the English sat 8 px higher). The new paragraph at a chapter is made in layout.js bandHTML.
- **Rule 3:** the mark comes from the verse's `brk` field (it must come over from the source and match Sefaria) and is set by `.band .brk`.
- **Rule 4:** scripts/layout.js gives each page its verses and comments; STRETCH = 0.15 (at 0.09 it split six more verses' comments). All fonts are loaded before measuring; the final check reports any overlap ("WARN overlap" / "WARN past bottom") — a draft with such a warning is not sent. After each build, check which page each verse's comments and notes land on and the empty space per page, and look at the pages as images.
- **Rule 5:** layout.js NB = 100 (was 440). render.py skips the chapter markers for the notes stream.
- **Rule 7:** each parasha's terms are in data/foot_terms.json (Bereshit's list is the starting point for the next parasha). The build warns "strip dropped" if a term is left out; then shorten a definition.
- **Rule 8:** set `title_page: true` and `title_en` in parasha.json; render.py builds the page and layout.js leaves out the on-page heading.
- **Rule 9:** always take page numbers, status and next_first_page from book.json (never from a skill or a note); see the commentary-book-build skill.
- **Rule 1 (size):** `.hc` in shared/scripts/book.css and bereshit/scripts/book.css: 15px on the 16.4px line grid; the bold headword `b.lem` 1.05em (a bigger headword or line height breaks the grid: make_pdf warns “non-grid block”). Held in book.json until Vayetze's final (review_round/apply_held_edits.py 111).
- **Rule 10:** the front matter lives in bereshit/data/front.json ('before'), built with `bereshit/scripts/render.py data/model_final5.json build/front.html frontonly`. "First:" places: small grey `.keytab .kf`; when a parasha becomes final, run review_round/key_add_parasha.py (term in the Hebrew commentary + house English in the English commentary of the same verse), read every candidate (review_round/key_verify.py) and add its first place to each entry.
- **Rule 11:** `bereshit/scripts/render.py data/model_final5.json build/back.html backonly`, numbered on from the last final page, running head GLOSSARY, written to `back/Ibn Ezra on the Torah - Glossary.pdf` by scripts/publish_pdfs.py every time the book is joined (front matter, final parashot, glossary). Terms from `<folder>/data/foot_terms.json`.
- **Rule 12:** layout.js warns "past bottom … (front/back matter)" when a static page's text runs below 980 px.
- **Rule 14:** make_pdf.py records the Chromium version in build/rep.json; book.json keeps each parasha's ("chromium"); check_all's pages check fails when they differ. A new parasha takes the version of its draft 1 and keeps it to the final. Bereshit, Noach and Lech Lecha were first laid out with 141 (Playwright 1.56), Vayera with 153 (Playwright 1.63).
- **Rule 15:** shared/ holds the scripts, fonts and hyphenation; each parasha's scripts/*.py is a stub that runs the shared script on its folder. The proof of the switch (same HTML apart from the font path, the same layout reports, all 152 pages the same picture for picture) is in shared/README.md. Rebuild each parasha a change touches with its Chromium version.
